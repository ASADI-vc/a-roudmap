# src-wef2025
- **Title:** Future of Jobs Report 2025 (5th edition; report URL redirects to https://www.weforum.org/reports/the-future-of-jobs-report-2025/)
- **Author / Organization:** World Economic Forum (Centre for the New Economy and Society; project team: Till Leopold, Attilio Di Battista, Ximena Játiva, Shuvasish Sharma, Ricky Li, Sam Grayling)
- **Canonical URL:** https://www.weforum.org/publications/the-future-of-jobs-report-2025/ (full PDF: https://www3.weforum.org/docs/WEF_Future_of_Jobs_Report_2025.pdf)
- **Publication / update date:** January 2025
- **Access date:** 2026-11-14
- **Source type:** employer survey
- **Research questions addressed:** RQ6 (employment/junior demand changes) primarily; secondary RQ7 context (which skills and technologies employers expect to matter, incl. AI)
- **Original summary (own words):** WEF's fifth biennial employer survey projects that structural labour-market transformation over 2025–2030 will touch 22% of today's total jobs: ~170 million jobs created (14% of current employment) offset by ~92 million displaced (8%), for net growth of 78 million (+7%). Technology roles dominate percentage growth — Big Data Specialists, FinTech Engineers, AI and Machine Learning Specialists, and Software and Applications Developers lead — while clerical roles (Cashiers and Ticket Clerks, Administrative Assistants, Postal Service Clerks, Bank Tellers, Data Entry Clerks) decline fastest. Employers expect 39% of workers' core skills to change by 2030, down from 44% in 2023, and skill gaps are the top-cited barrier to transformation.
- **Relevant findings:**
  - **Skill disruption:** "Overall, employers expect 39% of workers' core skills to change by 2030" (global survey of employers, fielded May–September 2024, published Jan 2025); "this measure of 'skill instability' has slowed compared to previous editions... from 44% in 2023 and a high point of 57% in 2020" (the 2016 edition's figure was 35%).
  - **Job creation/destruction (extrapolated from survey responses, 2025–2030, global):** creation equal to 14% of today's total employment = 170 million jobs; displacement of 8% = 92 million jobs; net growth 7% = 78 million jobs; total churn 22% of today's jobs.
  - **Fastest-growing roles in percentage terms (top of list, Fig 2.3):** Big Data Specialists, FinTech Engineers, AI and Machine Learning Specialists, Software and Applications Developers, Security Management Specialists, Data Warehousing Specialists, Autonomous and Electric Vehicle Specialists, UI and UX Designers, Light Truck or Delivery Services Drivers, Internet of Things Specialists, Data Analysts and Scientists, Environmental Engineers, Information Security Analysts, DevOps Engineer, Renewable Energy Engineers. Frontline roles (Farmworkers, Delivery Drivers, Construction Workers, Salespersons, Food Processing Workers) grow most in absolute volume.
  - **Fastest-declining roles:** "Clerical and Secretarial Workers – including Cashiers and Ticket Clerks, and Administrative Assistants and Executive Secretaries – are expected to see the largest decline in absolute numbers... fastest-declining roles to include Postal Service Clerks, Bank Tellers and Data Entry Clerks"; also Printing Workers and Accountants and Auditors named as fastest-declining in the body text (global, 2025–2030).
  - **Fastest-growing skills:** "AI and big data top the list as the fastest-growing skills, followed closely by networks and cybersecurity and technological literacy"; creative thinking and resilience, flexibility and agility also rising. Manual dexterity, endurance and precision shows its first-ever net decline (24% of respondents foresee decrease).
  - **Technology as driver:** 60% of employers expect broadening digital access to transform their business by 2030; "AI and information processing (86%); robotics and automation (58%); and energy generation, storage and distribution (41%)". AI and big data growth expectation exceeds 39% net in the top 10 industries; in Information and Technology Services, AI/big-data demand growth exceeds 39% (sector figures, 2025–2030).
  - **Workforce strategy numbers:** 50% of workers reported to have completed training/reskilling/upskilling vs 41% in 2023 edition; "if the world's workforce was made up of 100 people, 59 would need training by 2030... 29 could be upskilled in their current roles and 19 could be upskilled and redeployed... 11 would be unlikely to receive the reskilling or upskilling needed". Skill gaps cited as major barrier by 63% of employers (2025–2030); 85% plan to prioritize upskilling; 70% expect to hire staff with new skills; 40% plan to reduce staff whose skills become less relevant; 50% plan to transition staff from declining to growing roles.
  - **Iran note:** No mention of Iran anywhere in the fetched report text; Iran is not among the 55 surveyed economies.
- **Population / methodology / tools:** Future of Jobs Survey 2024 — "1,043 unique responses by global companies, collectively representing more than 14.1 million employees worldwide" across 22 industry clusters and 55 economies representing 88% of global GDP. Survey of 38 questions in 12 languages, fielded via Qualtrics from May to September 2024; target companies ≥500 employees ("largest multinational and national companies"). Occupation mapping via O*NET cross-walked with ISCO; skills via WEF Global Skills Taxonomy. Data partnerships with ADP, Coursera, Indeed and LinkedIn complement survey findings.
- **Limitations / conflicts of interest / mitigations / applicability:** Respondents are large, mostly leading/multinational employers recruited via WEF's network — self-selected, skewed to big firms; projections are employer expectations, not measured labour statistics, and are extrapolated to global job counts (170M/92M/78M are modelled extrapolations, "on current trends"). Occupations measured at role level, so junior-vs-senior dynamics within a role are not visible. WEF is a multistakeholder advocacy organization with an interest in promoting reskilling agendas. Figures for 2025–2030 are forecasts (uncertainty around GenAI is acknowledged by the report itself). Not Iran-specific; 55-economy coverage excludes Iran.
- **Candidate claims supported:**
  - observed (survey): 39% of workers' core skills expected to change by 2030, down from 44% in 2023 and 57% in 2020 (1,043 employer responses, 55 economies, fielded May–Sep 2024).
  - forecast: net +7% employment (+78M jobs) with 170M created / 92M displaced, 2025–2030, global (extrapolation from survey).
  - forecast: technology roles are fastest-growing in percentage terms — AI and Machine Learning Specialists and Software and Applications Developers among top 15; clerical roles (cashiers, bank tellers, data entry clerks) largest decline (global, 2025–2030).
  - survey: 86% of employers expect AI and information processing to transform their business by 2030; 60% expect broadening digital access to do so.
- **Implications for learners:** Software and application development remains a top-4 percentage-growth role through 2030, but demand is expected to concentrate in AI/ML, big data, cybersecurity and technological-literacy skills layered on top of programming; the 39% skill-instability figure and the 59/100-workers-need-training framing argue for continuous upskilling as a career strategy rather than one-time credentials. The 40% of employers planning staff reductions as skills become less relevant signals a premium on skills adjacent to growing roles, not shrinking ones.
