# src-cs2023
- **Title:** Computer Science Curricula 2023 (CS2023) — "CS2023: Curriculum for a Changing World"
- **Author / Organization:** The Joint Task Force on Computing Curricula: ACM, IEEE Computer Society (IEEE-CS), AAAI
- **Canonical URL:** https://cs2023.acm.org/ (NXDOMAIN at access date — subdomain no longer resolves; authoritative copies used: full report PDF https://ieeecs-media.computer.org/media/education/reports/CS2023.pdf and IEEE CS press release https://www.computer.org/press-room/new-cs2023-curriculum-guide)
- **Publication / update date:** January 2024 (title page); IEEE CS press release announcing the release dated 5 June 2024
- **Access date:** 2026-11-14
- **Source type:** curriculum standard (joint ACM/IEEE-CS/AAAI undergraduate CS curricular guidelines)
- **Research questions addressed:** RQ1 (durable foundations for AI-era engineers); indirectly RQ2 (foundations of programming as part of core)
- **Original summary (own words):** CS2023 is the joint ACM/IEEE-CS/AAAI undergraduate computer science curriculum guideline, produced by a global steering committee using a 2021 international survey and community feedback. It reorganizes the Body of Knowledge into 17 knowledge areas and splits required content into a deliberately minimal "CS Core" and depth options labeled "KA Core," letting programs pick coherent KA focuses. It replaces the older Tier model with a competency framework where competency combines knowledge, skills, and dispositions. Notable shifts for the AI era: an enlarged AI knowledge area, a dedicated chapter on Generative AI's implications for CS education, ethics/society content (SEP) integrated across KAs, and increased mathematics hours. The document explicitly frames professional dispositions and lifelong learning as graduate requirements.
- **Relevant findings:**
  - 17 knowledge areas (seen in report TOC/overview): Artificial Intelligence (AI); Algorithmic Foundations (AL); Architecture and Organization (AR); Data Management (DM); Foundations of Programming Languages (FPL); Graphics and Interactive Techniques (GIT); Human-Computer Interaction (HCI); Mathematical and Statistical Foundations (MSF); Networking and Communication (NC); Operating Systems (OS); Parallel and Distributed Computing (PDC); Software Development Fundamentals (SDF); Software Engineering (SE); Security (SEC); Society, Ethics, and the Profession (SEP); Systems Fundamentals (SF); Specialized Platform Development (SPD).
  - "The Body of Knowledge consisting of seventeen knowledge areas has been revised and updated"; "Topics that every graduate must know have been circumscribed as CS Core and kept to a minimum. Topics recommended for in-depth study have been labeled KA Core" (Introduction, "The discipline continues to evolve/grow" bullets).
  - Competency model: "Competency is defined as the sum of knowledge, skills, and dispositions" (IT2017 lineage); CS2023 treats competency as "a point in a 3D space with knowledge, skills, and dispositions as the three axes" (§Competence Model — Definitions and Terminology, ~p.41-42).
  - Per-KA core hours in the Body of Knowledge table (e.g., AL: 32 CS Core hours; AI: 12 CS Core + 18 KA Core hours; AR: 9; DM: 10) (Table 3 / Body of Knowledge chapter).
  - SEP: "issues of society, ethics, and the profession (SEP) have been explicitly enumerated in as many knowledge areas as possible" (SEP discussion); press release: "The SEP knowledge area is now an integral part of the curriculum."
  - Generative AI: "Generative AI is poised to impact computer science education. A chapter has been included that addresses how Generative AI could propel further innovation in computer science education" (Introduction); chapter "Generative AI and the Curriculum" (p.449) with implications by competency area; appendix "Generative AI in Introductory Programming" (p.438).
  - Press release (5 June 2024): release by IEEE CS with ACM and AAAI; 17-member global steering committee; 2021 global survey confirmed core areas "such as security, data management, and foundations of programming languages"; quote from steering co-chair Rajendra K. Raj (RIT).
  - Graduates framed as "Self-directed, as commitment to life-long learning is required due to rapid evolution" (competency dispositions discussion).
- **Population / methodology / tools:** Consensus curriculum-development process: joint task force, 17-member steering committee, 2021 global survey of academics/industry, community review and feedback; knowledge-area chapters each with CS Core/KA Core hours and competency examples.
- **Limitations / conflicts of interest / applicability:** Professional-society consensus document — reflects negotiated norms, not empirical outcome data; hours allocations are recommendations, not laws. The original cs2023.acm.org site was unreachable (NXDOMAIN), so citations rely on the IEEE CS-hosted PDF (identical title-page content verified). Applicability: strong baseline for what the field agrees every CS/SE graduate should know; doesn't prescribe pedagogy or study techniques (RQ3 out of scope).
- **Candidate claims supported:**
  - observed — CS2023 defines 17 knowledge areas with a minimal CS Core plus KA Core depth options, replacing the Tier-1/Tier-2 scheme of CS2013.
  - observed — CS2023 adopts a competency framework combining knowledge, skills, and dispositions.
  - observed — CS2023 adds Generative AI material (dedicated chapter p.449 and appendix on GenAI in introductory programming) and integrates SEP ethics content across knowledge areas.
  - recommendation — programs should let graduates develop professional dispositions including lifelong learning and proactive attention to usability/security/ethics.
- **Implications for learners:** The consensus "durable core" for AI-era engineers is fundamentals-heavy (algorithms, programming languages, systems, math, security) plus ethics and AI literacy; tool-specific AI skills are treated as fast-changing context (via the GenAI chapter), not as replacement for fundamentals.
